# Mars terrain and route assessment

The Cloudflare workflow builds the root Vite app. The live site's JavaScript referenced the root `globalRoute.worker`; the separate `website/` demo still contains preset geometric paths and metrics. The root planner previously used a 0.25° grid of Mars Orbiter Laser Altimeter (MOLA) elevation outside two detailed crops. That is about 14.8 km at the equator, enough to smooth many slopes and small landforms away. The old display also rounded corners independently of the solver.

## Terrain inputs and route costs

Wildcat Ridge uses the bundled 1 m Perseverance-area [HiRISE stereo elevation](https://doi.org/10.5066/P9REJ9JN). The Mount Sharp pack uses a 20 m sample from the [USGS Gale elevation mosaic](https://astrogeology.usgs.gov/search/map/mars_msl_gale_merged_dem_1m). Elsewhere the worker tries a bounded 256 × 256 Mars Trek GeoTIFF from the smallest applicable coverage window, including the [200 m USGS HRSC/MOLA blend](https://astrogeology.usgs.gov/search/map/mars_mgs_mola_mex_hrsc_blended_dem_global_200m). Displayed spacing accounts for both the export and source spacing. Global MOLA remains available for long journeys and when a suitable crop cannot be loaded. A failed route from a loaded crop is reported at that resolution. The planner only falls back when the crop itself is unavailable. Each leg uses one elevation source. There is no automatic stitching or refinement of multi-region routes.

A* searches eight neighboring cells. Missing elevation and reported blocked cells are excluded, and diagonals cannot cut the corner of a missing cell. Edges above the chosen absolute grade are rejected. A route through a single regional cell cannot establish traversal between distinct locations. Distances come from the geodesic lengths of the calculated polyline on the Mars 2000 reference sphere. This is horizontal distance, not a wheel-odometer reading. The endpoint inside each accepted cell uses that cell's representative elevation.

Segment cost is horizontal distance × (1 + weight × terrain allowance factor), in distance units. The factor is the squared slope angle divided by the squared mission slope limit, weighted one on ascents and half on descents. It also includes 0.25 × the roughness index for DEMs with effective spacing of 5 m or finer. Roughness measures local elevation residual after removing a linear slope across each axis, normalized by half the cell spacing and capped at one. The roughness allowance uses the average of both cells; missing roughness neighborhoods use the maximum proxy allowance and are reported as unresolved coverage. This is a terrain effort proxy. It does not identify rocks, soil traction, or small obstacles.

Route weights are 0 (shortest feasible grid path), 1 (balanced), and 4 (lower terrain cost candidate). The recommendation must improve equivalent distance by more than 1% to justify switching from the shortest. A lower terrain candidate must cut the allowance by more than 1% and more than 0.5% of shortest distance. Both alternatives are capped at 25% added distance, per leg. These thresholds and weights are planning assumptions. This compares the optima of three weighted objectives; it does not enumerate every potentially useful path. The recommended estimate divides horizontal distance plus terrain allowance by the selected base speed in m/s. Only detailed routes receive a time estimate or time budget. Science stops and the stated return reserve are accounted for separately. Actual speed, oxygen, energy, and consumables require mission-specific models.

The 2D and 3D lines share the computed vertices and the existing registered HiRISE image at close zoom over Wildcat Ridge. The 3D line samples the rendered terrain along each segment for height. The local slope overlay shows the steepest adjacent measured step in the two bundled regions, in degrees. The Wildcat Ridge roughness overlay uses the same normalized residual as the route planner. Gray or transparent regions have unknown coverage.

## Environmental evidence

- [NASA Curiosity RAD](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/radiation-measurements-on-mars/) and the [PDS RAD archive specification](https://pds-ppi.igpp.ucla.edu/volume/MSLRAD_1002/DOCUMENT/RAD_RDR_SIS.PDF) describe time-tagged instrument observations. [Charpentier et al.](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025JE009336) compared neutral-radiation observations with site-specific particle transport modeling in Gale. [Guo et al.](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026JE009704) used observed parking intervals, DEM-derived sky obstruction, solar modulation and a zenith-dependent model to separate sky and surface contributions. The papers support regional research using those inputs, but their sampled sites do not supply a validated mission dose-rate raster for the routes offered here. A future model would need site-specific terrain horizons, atmospheric and GCR conditions, secondary radiation, a mission shielding and detector/dose definition, and calibration against matched RAD periods. Integrate any validated rate against segment duration in matching units. The app leaves exposure unevaluated and applies no radiation cost.
- NASA Mars Trek Odyssey THEMIS day/night infrared images are historical observations. The accompanying thermal-inertia product describes heat retention; it does not represent current surface temperature. A route penalty would require local season, time, surface conditions, and a mission thermal model. These layers remain context.
- The Mars Global Surveyor TES Dust Cover Index describes historical relative surface dust. It cannot resolve active dust storms, current atmospheric opacity, terrain bearing strength, or local traction. These are unevaluated hazards.

## Globe asset

[John Davies' Mars Terrain Model](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/mars-terrain-model-e769bb157de04913b1c8b95e2ff2a60d) uses the USGS HRSC/MOLA blend. Sketchfab metadata reports 900,225 vertices, 1,799,999 faces, roughly 5 km input sampling, 20× exaggerated relief, and [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). The authorized download endpoint returned HTTP 401. The exported file is needed to inspect format, textures or vertex colors, the mesh's global extent, coordinate axes, and memory and rendering cost.

Once the original export is available, fit a rotation, sphere center, radius and signed radial scale against the source DEM. Verify with Olympus Mons, Valles Marineris, Hellas Planitia and Jezero, using coordinate and elevation residuals and checking the antimeridian and poles. Generate UVs in the explorer's east-positive, planetocentric convention. Only reverse the 20× relief after verifying the scale and datum. Benchmark a simplified mesh and lazy loading, retain the detailed regional terrain, and credit the author and preserve ShareAlike for a distributed adaptation. If the mesh cannot be registered, the georeferenced USGS blend can provide a replacement visual surface while the routing DEMs remain independent.

## Verification

The root production build and all 43 automated tests passed with the Earth Walk changes from upstream. Local Edge checks at 1440 × 960 and 390 × 844 covered the route choices, 2D/3D switching, slope and roughness layer controls, and entering and exiting Recorded Walk. Physical mobile sensors and GPU performance were outside this check.

A Wildcat Ridge round trip using 1 m HiRISE elevation produced three choices. The recommended route measured about 442 m, with a peak step of 14.8° under a 15° limit. The lower terrain cost path measured about 453 m. Valles Marineris used one 200 m HRSC/MOLA crop request for both legs and returned a 48.1 km regional round trip. Blocking NASA Mars Trek requests verified fallback to the bundled MOLA source.
